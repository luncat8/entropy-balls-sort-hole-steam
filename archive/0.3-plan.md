# 0.3 — Hole force, energy and controls

## Outcome

Each participant has an independent energy store and a visible force panel. A human controls the hole with a signed vertical slider or by holding the field; bot requests use the same funding rules and appear in a read-only panel. The initial game has one human; participant and bot selection are specified separately in plan 2.0.

## Design

- `force.js` owns each participant's selected direction and magnitude, hold state, stored energy, funded contribution and the combined drive. Stores start at capacity 100, recharge 10 units/second, and a full request costs 25 units/second. Capacity and cost are adjustable through their owner. At each fixed step, recharge up to capacity, then pay the cost of the held, nonzero request. Scale a contribution to the energy available that step. Zero cost permits free force; zero direction or an idle control spends nothing. Requests pay even when the hole is blocked or an opposing request cancels them. Sum funded signed contributions and clamp to −1…1.
- `forcepanel.js` builds one labeled panel per registered participant and rebuilds it when the participant list or controller type changes. The green midpoint is zero, above it selects upward force, below it downward force, and distance from the midpoint determines the magnitude. Show the selected value, whether force is being applied, and the actual funded contribution. Bot panels are read-only and show their request and contribution through the same view as human panels.
- The vertical energy meter shows stored energy; its red portion previews the gross cost of a two-second hold at the current request, capped visually at the energy currently stored. Display the full estimated cost separately, including fractional units, and make clear that recharge continues during a hold. Update values when they change and expose the slider and meter to assistive technology.
- The human slider starts at 60% down. Hold or drag it to apply its signed force; releasing retains the selection but stops the contribution. The wheel over the field or the player's panel moves the selection in 5% signed steps without starting a hold. With the field or slider focused, up/down keys adjust the selection and holding Space applies it.
- Holding the canvas uses the absolute slider magnitude and chooses direction from the pointer's vertical position relative to the hole. Convert CSS pointer coordinates through the canvas backing scale and letterboxing, clamp targets to the track, and re-evaluate direction before every fixed step. A half-step dead zone avoids oscillation and energy consumption on reaching a stationary target. Releasing the canvas restores the slider's selected direction. Slider, canvas and keyboard holds are independent, with the canvas direction taking priority while it is held.
- Capture active pointers; pointer release, cancellation, lost capture, window blur, document hiding and controller handoff clear the corresponding hold. Other held sources remain active until released. Changing a selection without a hold never applies force.
- Optional hole friction is off by default. With it enabled, the signed velocity is `drive * maxSpeed / (1 + k)`, where `k = 1`, and the opposing friction contribution is proportional to that velocity. This overdamped model has no residual motion when force is released. Hitting a track endpoint clamps movement; resistance is zero when the hole cannot move. Friction changes speed, not the energy price of the request.
- `settings.js` owns the friction checkbox and the `friction=1` URL parameter. Omit the default, preserve other query parameters and the hash, and use `history.replaceState`. If a file URL rejects replacement, show a copyable URL that restores the setting. Settings collapse initially on narrow screens so they do not cover the quest; compact panels preserve field space on short screens.
- A held quest freezes simulation and energy while the panel reports the pause. The goal preview releases on pointer cancellation, lost capture, focus loss or window blur.

## Files and ownership

- `force.js` — requests, stores, funding and force views; `forcepanel.js` — participant panels and energy preview.
- `input.js` — human gestures and selection; `players.js` — participant identity and controller type.
- `simulation.js` — speed-proportional friction, fixed-step control hook, hole movement and bounds; `canvas.js` — coordinate conversion.
- `settings.js` — friction setting and URL; `main.js` and `index.html` — wiring and shell.

## Validation

- L0: `node --check` on touched JavaScript.
- L1: signed slider, field targeting, wheel/keyboard/touch holds and all release paths; two independent requests, cancellation, funding limits, zero direction, recharge, endpoints, friction and two-second meter segment; bot and human panels; URL round trip and file URL fallback.
- L2: run the available simulation, task, quest-preview and force smokes plus the difficulty sweep. Manually inspect desktop and narrow layouts, press and drag the slider, hold the canvas above and below the hole, change force with the wheel, toggle friction, reload a copied URL, and confirm goal-preview pause and release. Verify a registered bot's panel shows its actual funded force and independent energy.

## Dependencies and next step

The field and participant/task interfaces come from 0.1 and 0.2. Expand settings and status through gui.1; implement selectable second participants, local two-human controls and bot behavior through 2.0.
